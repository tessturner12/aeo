# The AEO Playbook: A 30-Day Plan to Build a Measurement Business

**Target brand:** Mighty Accounting (mightyaccounting.com)
**Competitor set:** Gorilla Accounting, GoForma, Crunch, QAccounting, Caroola (SJD)
**Niche:** UK contractor/freelancer limited company accountancy

---

# Part 0: What This Business Actually Is

## The one-paragraph version

When someone asks ChatGPT "what's the best accountant for a UK contractor running a limited company?", ChatGPT names two or three firms. Being one of those named firms is worth real client revenue to those companies. Nobody reliably knows how to become one of the named firms, and — more importantly — almost nobody can *measure* whether they are one. You are going to build the measuring instrument, use it to run a real experiment on a real accountancy firm, and sell the answer.

## Why this exists at all (the shift, explained from zero)

For twenty years, the way people found products or services was: type words into Google, get a list of ten blue links, click one. An entire industry (SEO — search engine optimization) grew up around getting your link into that list of ten.

That's changing. People increasingly ask an AI assistant instead, and the assistant gives them *one answer* with a handful of firms named in it. There's no list of ten. There's a paragraph that says "the main options are X, Y, and Z."

If you're firm Z, that's everything. If you're firm Q, you don't exist.

The new discipline of getting named is called **AEO** — Answer Engine Optimization. (You'll also see **GEO**, Generative Engine Optimization. Practitioners use them roughly interchangeably; don't get hung up on it.)

## How the machine actually works under the hood

This part matters because it explains why the job is winnable at all.

Early AI models (2022-2023) were trained on a frozen snapshot of the internet, often a year or more old. You couldn't influence them — by the time your content was in the training data, a year had passed. Optimizing against a 1+ year feedback loop is close to impossible.

That's not how it works now. Modern AI answers use something called **RAG** — Retrieval-Augmented Generation. It's a two-step process:

1. **Retrieval:** You ask "what's the best accountant for an IT contractor?" The system runs an actual web search behind the scenes and pulls back a set of pages.
2. **Generation:** The AI reads those pages and writes a summary answer, citing some of them.

This is the single most important fact in this entire document. **The AI isn't remembering a firm from training. It's reading a set of web pages, right now, and summarising them.**

Two consequences:

- **The feedback loop is fast.** Change a page today, the AI might read it tomorrow. That's what makes this a real job rather than astrology.
- **The battlefield is the retrieval set.** The pages the AI pulls back are the entire universe of what it knows in that moment. If Mighty isn't mentioned on any of those pages, Mighty cannot be in the answer. It's not a ranking problem. It's a *presence in the retrieved documents* problem.

## Owned vs earned — and why this isn't SEO with a new hat

In old SEO, you fought to get *your page* ranking. That's "owned" — you control it.

In AEO, the AI retrieves maybe 8-10 pages and blends them. At best, one of those is yours. The other nine are third parties — comparison directories (ContractorUK, UmbrellaCompany, LimitedCompanyHelp), review sites, forums, competitors' own "best accountant" content. To actually win the answer, you need to be *mentioned on other people's pages*. That's "earned."

There's a rule of thumb worth internalising: **the more general the question, the more it's an earned game; the more specific the question, the more it's an owned game.** Ask "best accountant" and you get directories and roundups, not accounting firms themselves. Ask something narrow and specific and the actual firms start appearing.

This has a direct implication for Mighty: you will not win "best UK accountant" with content on mightyaccounting.com. You might win "accountant for a small consultancy operating through a limited company, no VC funding, one fixed fee."

## Product Questions — the filter that saves you a wasted month

Here's a distinction that most people entering this space miss entirely.

Ask an AI "how do I register a limited company?" You get helpful information. **No firms are named.** No firm will ever be named for that exact question. Optimizing for it is worth precisely zero, forever.

Ask "which accountants specialise in IT contractors and IR35?" and firms appear.

Questions where firms appear in the answer are called **Product Questions**. (The term comes from ecommerce/SaaS AEO research, but it applies just as well to services — think of it as "Service Questions" if that reads more naturally.) Everything else is a decoy. Research on this consistently finds that most informational/how-to questions don't surface named providers, while comparison and recommendation questions do — but you must verify per-niche, never assume.

**This is your Day 2 filter and it's ruthless.** A question that never surfaces named firms is not a small opportunity. It's a zero. Delete it.

## What you are actually selling

Not content. Not schema markup. Not an audit PDF.

**You are selling a measurement instrument and the honest answer it produces.**

The pitch to Mighty: *"Right now, when contractors ask AI which accountant to use, you don't come up and four of your competitors do. I've measured it — across four AI platforms, across sixty phrasings of the question, five times each, with error bars. Now let's change something on half of it, hold the other half as a control, and find out what actually moves. I'll tell you honestly if nothing does."*

That last sentence is the whole business.

## Why "I'll tell you honestly if nothing does" is the moat

The AEO market right now is full of people selling certainty about a discipline nobody has figured out. The most credible voice in the space — Ethan Smith at Graphite, who runs a growth agency working with Webflow, Adobe and Upwork and teaches this at Reforge — says openly that we're in early exploration mode and no one actually knows what the most impactful things are.

Meanwhile the market is producing confident case studies at volume. Smith flags four specific ways those case studies are fake, usually sincerely:

| Trap | What it looks like | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **Misattribution** | "ChatGPT-referred enquiries up 50% since we started!" | LLM usage is growing rapidly on its own. If usage grows 50% and you did nothing, you can still claim the win. |
| **Relative vs absolute** | "900% increase in AI referrals!" | 1 enquiry/month → 10/month. Meaningless. |
| **Vanity metrics** | "We now appear for 400 questions!" | Appearances aren't enquiries. Enquiries aren't signed clients. |
| **Fancy brands** | "We do AEO for [Huge Firm]" | Working with them ≠ moving anything for them. |

The fix for the first one — and it's the fix that defines your entire offer — is a **control group.** Change half, leave half alone, compare the two. If both went up, the market went up and you did nothing.

Nobody in this market is doing that. You will. That's the business.

## Your specific unfair advantage

Be clear-eyed about why you, specifically:

1. **You measure things for a living.** This is a sampling-and-variance problem wearing a marketing costume. Share of Answers is a *rate with a confidence interval*, not a count. That's your day job.
2. **You know the buyer AND the product from the inside.** You operate outside IR35 through your own limited company, Tess Turner Ltd. You've run the actual month-end workflow — timesheets, TfL commute expenses, invoicing, Pandle bookkeeping, DLA settlement, corporation tax provisioning, dividend withdrawal. You know exactly what a contractor searching for an accountant is worried about, because you've lived the thing the accountant handles. There is no keyword volume data for AI questions — the platforms have no ad products yet, so nobody can look it up. Everyone else is guessing. You're not.
3. **You have an audience of exactly this buyer.** Your Instagram covers contracting life and your limited company alongside data careers and AI tools. This case study is content for your own audience, not adjacent to it.

## What this is not

- **Not a get-rich-quick thing.** The video that started this is one of about fifteen near-identical "7 Claude AI Side Hustles" videos published in the last few weeks, and it's top-of-funnel for a paid course. The *idea* is real; the framing is a funnel.
- **Not guaranteed to work.** You may finish 30 days having proven nothing moved. That's a real outcome, it's *still publishable*, and it would make you one of very few people with a rigorous negative finding in a field drowning in fake positives.
- **Not for pretending you're certain.** The certainty is the product's opposite.

## The honest state of the market (pricing)

Directionally, from published sources — every one of which is content marketing written by someone selling AEO, so weight accordingly:

- Entry-level programs: ~$1,000-$2,500/month
- Mid-market retainers: ~$2,000-$8,000/month
- Independent consultants: ~$100-$150/hour
- Enterprise: $10,000-$25,000+/month

The spread is absurd — some agencies quote $1,000 while others demand $15,000 for arguably the same work — and at least one industry guide states plainly that a chunk of the "AEO" tier is traditional SEO shops that relabelled their pricing page. Nobody publishes rate cards. The market hasn't settled.

**A separate, useful data point for this specific niche:** there's already a small cottage industry of agencies (GetVisus, Renownly, Tramwai and others) selling "AI visibility for accountants" specifically, and they describe the UK accountancy sector as having almost no AI visibility strategy anywhere — the opportunity is described as "almost entirely uncontested." That's a genuine tailwind: you're not explaining a novel idea to this niche, you're arriving slightly ahead of an already-forming market.

That's *good* for someone arriving with an actual instrument.

## What's different, doing this for a service business rather than a SaaS product

Two adjustments worth having in your head from day one, because the plan below was written generically and these are the accountancy-specific edges:

- **Location matters.** "Best contractor accountant in Manchester" and "best contractor accountant" (no location) can retrieve different pages and produce different answers. Mighty and most of its competitors serve UK-wide remotely, so you'll mostly be testing location-agnostic and "remote/online" phrasings — but include a couple of location variants to check whether local directories dominate those retrieval sets differently.
- **The buyer is more risk-averse than a software buyer.** Nobody picks an accountant on a whim — trust signals (qualifications, years trading, client testimonials, professional body membership) matter more in the answer than in, say, a job-app tool comparison. Expect "sentiment" and "trust cues named" to matter as much as raw presence when you're designing interventions in Week 2.

# Part 1: Why Mighty

## The selection logic, so you can repeat it

Five criteria, in priority order:

**1. Does the category surface named firms at all?**
UK contractor accountancy: yes. Every roundup and directory names specific firms — Gorilla Accounting, GoForma, QAccounting, Caroola/SJD, Brookson, Crunch, Clear House, Clever Accounts. Gate passed. There's also, unusually for a services category, already a small industry of agencies specifically pitching "AI visibility for accountants" — further confirmation the category is live.

**2. Is there headroom?**
This kills most candidates. Caroola/SJD (a merger of two of the biggest legacy contractor accountancy brands) appears in nearly every roundup — saturated, no room to move, you'd be measuring a ceiling. Same with Crunch and GoForma, both of which show up repeatedly across ContractorUK, UmbrellaCompany, and LimitedCompanyHelp directories.

Mighty is **absent from the major roundup and directory lists** despite being a real, well-reviewed, multi-year operating firm. That's a floor with room above it. That's an experiment.

**3. Is it clean enough to publish?**
Mighty: bootstrapped, customer-funded, no VC. Run by named founders (Mark and James) with the CEO's personal email and mobile number publicly listed on the "About" page, explicitly inviting contact. Positive reviews, no BBB-style horror stories, most new clients arrive via referral — which is itself evidence the underlying service is good; they simply haven't translated word-of-mouth trust into AI-answer visibility.

**4. Is the founder reachable?**
About as reachable as a founder gets without you already knowing them. A personal mobile number on a public "About" page, with an open invitation to "chat, complain, or share suggestions," is a much lower barrier than a generic "contact us" form or a busy YC founder fielding pitches daily.

**5. Are the question clusters distinct?**
"Small, mighty, one fixed fee, no VC, customer-funded, real accountant support" is a genuinely differentiated position against the "we're the biggest contractor specialist" framing that Caroola/SJD and Crunch lean on. That gives you clean, narrow clusters to target — you're not fighting for the generic "best UK accountant" head term, you're fighting for the "small firm, fixed fee, direct access to your accountant" niche within the niche.

## Where Mighty is a genuinely better pick than the alternatives considered

Two brands were seriously evaluated and rejected before Mighty, and it's worth knowing why, because the same logic will help you evaluate client two and three later:

- **Sorce (a "Tinder for jobs" app)** was rejected because it had almost no indexable web content — a mobile app with a landing page has nothing to run a content experiment *on*. No pages to enhance, no test/control split possible.
- **Huntr (a job-tracking platform)** was rejected because it already runs a sophisticated in-house content/SEO operation (a documented 5,000% organic traffic case study, a named Head of Partnerships and Product Marketing Manager). Pitching "let me help with your content visibility" to a team already doing that well is a much harder sell.

Mighty avoids both problems: it has a real website with real content to work with, and — unlike Huntr — nobody is currently running a content strategy for it at all. That's the sweet spot: enough substance to experiment on, and a genuine gap nobody's filling.

## Where I'd bet against myself

Three honest risks, stated plainly:

**Mighty may simply be too small for the retrieval sets you're measuring.** A firm with a handful of staff and a client base built on referrals may have thin third-party citation surface (few reviews, few mentions on comparison sites) — meaning even a successful intervention has less to work with than a firm like Gorilla or GoForma which already has some presence to build from.

→ *Day 1 test:* as part of the manual check, specifically note whether Mighty is mentioned *anywhere*, even negatively or in passing, across your five test questions. Zero mentions across all four platforms on all runs tells you the starting baseline is close to the statistical floor — worth knowing before you commit further.

**The buyer is trust-sensitive, so the levers may be slower to move than for a SaaS tool.** Getting an accountant recommended by AI may hinge more on being present in trust-signal-heavy content (professional body pages, verified review platforms) than on blog volume — and building genuine third-party trust signals takes longer than publishing a comparison page.

**A five-to-ten-person accountancy firm may not have marketing budget or bandwidth**, even if the founder is warm and reachable. Genuine willingness to chat is not the same as budget to pay a retainer. Keep the ask small and the value obviously free at first.

# Part 2: Where This Runs (and what it costs)

Unchanged from the general plan — worth restating briefly since it's brand-agnostic infrastructure.

## The recommendation

**Build locally (Days 1-5, SQLite, free). Move to a Hetzner CX22 (~€4.50/month, hourly billed) before Day 15,** when the experiment needs to run unattended overnight. Both laptops connect to the same box via VS Code Remote-SSH, so switching machines mid-project isn't a sync problem — there's one database, one source of truth, on the server.

A USB drive was considered and rejected: it solves file-syncing, not scheduling, and the rig's core requirement — running unattended at 3am for 30 consecutive days — needs a machine that's always on, not a file you carry between laptops.

# Part 3: Week 1 — Build the Instrument (Days 1-7)

**Goal by Sunday:** a rig that answers *"how often does Mighty appear for question-cluster X, on surface Y, and how noisy is that number?"*

## Day 1: Verify the category (2 hours, no code)

Before writing a line of code, prove the opportunity is real. Open ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Gemini. Ask, by hand, in fresh/logged-out sessions where possible:

1. "What's the best accountant for a UK contractor running a limited company?"
2. "I run a small consultancy through a limited company — who should do my accounts?"
3. "Best accountant for IT contractors and IR35 2026?"
4. "I want a fixed-fee accountant for my one-person limited company, not a big impersonal firm — any recommendations?"
5. "What's the easiest way to find a good, affordable accountant for a freelancer's limited company?"

For each, write down: **which firms got named, in what order, and which URLs got cited.**

You're checking three things:
- **Do named firms appear?** (If no → abandon the niche. Very unlikely given the research already done, but check.)
- **Does Mighty appear anywhere?** (Expected: almost never. That's the point — but note it precisely, including any near-misses like being cited on a review aggregator without being a headline recommendation.)
- **Which firms dominate, and via which URLs?** This is your competitor map and your citation-source map in one pass — write down every distinct domain that gets cited (ContractorUK, UmbrellaCompany, LimitedCompanyHelp, GoForma's own blog, etc.), because that list becomes Week 2's intervention target.

Also: **ask each question twice.** You will get different answers. That's not a bug — it's the single most important property of this entire domain, and it's why Day 3 exists.

**Deliverable:** a page of notes. Go/no-go decision made explicitly.

## Day 2: Question research (4-6 hours, no code)

The hard part. There's no tool for this — question research tools don't exist yet because **there's no search volume data for AI questions.** Keyword volume in SEO comes from Google's ad products; AI platforms have no ad products yet, so there's no truth set. Everyone is guessing.

You guess better than most because you've been both the buyer and, in effect, the product — you've done your own month-end accounting workflow for your own limited company.

### The method

**Step 1 — Brain dump (45 min).** Write every question you'd have typed into ChatGPT if you'd been shopping for an accountant instead of doing it yourself — and every genuine worry you had setting up and running Tess Turner Ltd (IR35 status, dividend vs salary, what expenses are allowable, corporation tax provisioning, whether you needed an accountant at all vs DIY on Pandle). Don't filter. Your own experience is primary research.

**Step 2 — Expand with Claude (30 min).** For each seed, ask Claude to generate 20 phrasings a real person might use. Phrasings matter enormously — you're measuring across a *distribution* of ways people ask, not one string.

**Step 3 — Cluster into topics (1 hour).** A **topic** is a cluster of questions sharing one intent. Aim for 6-8 topics, ~60 questions total.

Likely clusters for Mighty:
- General "best accountant for contractors" (broad, competitive)
- IR35-specific advice and compliance
- Fixed-fee / small firm / no big-agency-feel positioning ← *Mighty's natural home*
- Switching accountants (mid-year, dissatisfaction with a current provider)
- Dividend vs salary / tax efficiency questions
- Software/platform questions (Xero, FreeAgent, Pandle compatibility)
- New limited company setup (first-time contractors)
- Freelancer/agency-specific (not just solo contractors — Mighty explicitly serves "small agencies" too)

**Step 4 — Tier each question.**
- **Head:** "best accountant for a UK limited company" — high volume, brutal competition
- **Mid:** "best accountant for IT contractors" — moderate both
- **Long:** "fixed-fee accountant for a one-person consultancy who wants to actually speak to a real accountant, not a call centre" — low volume, low competition, *most winnable*, and closest to Mighty's actual positioning

**Step 5 — THE PRODUCT QUESTION FILTER.** For each question, ask it once in Perplexity. Named firms appear? Tag `is_product = TRUE`. No named firms? **Delete the row.** Not "deprioritise." Delete.

This step will kill 30-40% of your list — informational questions like "how do I register a limited company" or "what is IR35" will produce excellent, helpful, entirely firm-free answers. That's the step working correctly.

**Deliverable:** `questions.csv` — `id, text, topic, tier, is_product, notes` (schema and code covered in the appendix, when we write it).
